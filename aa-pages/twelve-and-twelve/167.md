      “Alcoholics Anonymous should remain forever
      nonprofessional, but our service centers may
      employ special workers.”


ALCOHOLICS ANONYMOUS will never have a pro-
fessional class. We have gained some understanding of the
ancient words “Freely ye have received, freely give.” We
have discovered that at the point of professionalism, mon-
ey and spirituality do not mix. Almost no recovery from
alcoholism has ever been brought about by the world’s best
professionals, whether medical or religious. We do not de-
cry professionalism in other fields, but we accept the sober
fact that it does not work for us. Every time we have tried
to professionalize our Twelfth Step, the result has been ex-
actly the same: Our single purpose has been defeated.
   Alcoholics simply will not listen to a paid twelfth-stepper.
Almost from the beginning, we have been positive that face-
to-face work with the alcoholic who suffers could be based
only on the desire to help and be helped. When an A.A. talks
for money, whether at a meeting or to a single newcomer, it
can have a very bad effect on him, too. The money motive
compromises him and everything he says and does for his
prospect. This has always been so obvious that only a very
few A.A.’s have ever worked the Twelfth Step for a fee.
