  No, I didn’t go home, but found work not too far away. I still drank. I soon
got restless and took a freight for a Michigan city, arriving there broke late at
night. I set out to look for friends. They helped me find work. Slowly I began
to climb the industrial job ladder once more and eventually achieved a respon-
sible position as a machine setter in a large plant. I was sitting on top of the
world again. The sense of accomplishment I had now told me that I had earned
the right to have enjoyable week-ends once more. The week-ends began to
extend to Tuesday and Wednesday until I frequently worked only from Thurs-
day to Saturday with the bottle always in my mind. In a vague sort of way I had
set a time to quit drinking but that was at least fifteen years away and ”What
the hell!“ I said to myself. ”I’m going to have a good time while I’m young.“
  Then I was fired. Piqued, I drank up my last pay check and when I got sober
