I didn’t find it necessary to patronize the same place each evening. It doesn’t
      pay to be seen in the same place at the same hour every day, you know.
         The general procedure was to take 4 or 5 shots in the first place I stopped at.
      This would get me feeling fit, and then I would start for home and fireside,
      thirteen miles away. Well . . . on the way home numerous places must be passed.
      If I were alone I would stop at four or five of them, but only one or two in the
      event I had my mistrusting wife with me.
         Eventually I would arrive home for a late supper, for which, of course, I had
      absolutely no relish. I would make a feeble attempt at eating supper but never
      met with any howling success. I never enjoyed any meal, but I ate my lunch at
      noon for two reasons: first, to help get me out of the fog of the night before,
      and second, to furnish some measure of nourishment. (My enjoyment of meals
      now is an added feature to the Seven Wonders of the World to me. I can still
      hardly believe it). Eventually, the noon meal was also dispensed with.
         I cannot remember just when I became the victim of insomnia, but I do
      know that the last year and a half I never went to bed sober a single night. I
      couldn’t sleep. I had a mortal fear of going to bed and tossing all night. Eve-
      nings at home were an ordeal. As a result, I would fall off in a drunken stupor
      every night.
