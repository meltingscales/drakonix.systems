self in his place. Let him see that you want to be helpful rather than critical.
  When a discussion does arise, you might suggest he read this book, or at
least the chapter on alcoholism. Tell him you have been worried, though per-
haps needlessly. You think he ought to know the subject better, as everyone
should have a clear understanding of the risk he takes if he drinks too much.
Show him you have confidence in his power to stop or moderate. Say you do
not want to be a wet blanket; that you only want him to take care of his health.
Thus you may succeed in interesting him in alcoholism.
  He probably has several alcoholics among his own acquaintances. You might
suggest that you both take an interest in them. Drinkers like to help other drink-
ers. Your husband may be willing to talk to one of them.
  If this kind of approach does not catch your husband’s interest, it may be
best to drop the subject, but after a friendly talk your husband will usually
revive the topic himself. This may take patient waiting, but it will be worth it.
Meanwhile you might try to help the wife of another serious drinker. If you act
upon these principles, your husband may stop or moderate.
