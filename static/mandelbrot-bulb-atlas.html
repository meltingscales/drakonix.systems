The bulbs of the Mandelbrot set

Each coloured region is a hyperbolic component: a bulb where z → z² + c settles into a cycle of a fixed period. Everything on this page is computed live in your browser. Click the map to zoom in; hover to read the period of any point.

hover the map

Read the labels as fractions. The bulb touching the main cardioid at angle p/q (in turns) has period q. Going around the cardioid, the bulbs follow the order of fractions, and between two bulbs the largest one is their mediant. For example, between 1/3 and 1/2 the largest bulb is 2/5 = (1+1)/(3+2). This is the Farey sequence, the same object the Franel–Landau theorem ties to the Riemann hypothesis. Here it shows up just because bulbs are labelled by rational numbers.

Every bulb center of period n

The centers of the period-n bulbs are the roots of the polynomial fcn(0), which has degree 2n−1. A Web Worker finds all of its roots at once with the Aberth–Ehrlich method, then sorts each root by its exact period. Dots of lower periods appear because their periods divide n.

"Predicted" uses the Möbius-style count: 2n−1 minus the centers of every smaller period dividing n. When n is prime, only period 1 is removed, leaving 2n−1 − 1. Whether the polynomial for the exact-period-n centers (the Gleason polynomial) is always irreducible over the integers is still open. Manning checked it for n ≤ 10; Buff, Floyd, Koch and Parry studied it modulo 2; Mihalache and Vigneron have computed centers up to period 41.

The road to the Feigenbaum point

The bulbs of period 1, 2, 4, 8, … shrink toward a single point on the real axis. Newton's method finds each center; the ratio of successive gaps converges to Feigenbaum's constant, δ ≈ 4.669201609.

PeriodCenter cGap ratio

This cascade sits at the frontier of current research. Dudko and Lyubich proved the MLC conjecture (local connectivity of the set) at Feigenbaum points, a case that had been open for decades. Their proof also explains why this ratio is universal.
